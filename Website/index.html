Mulvium in Brief

Good ideas deserve institutions.

01 · Mission

Turn ideas into institutions.

Mulvium builds the companies that carry an idea the whole distance: from the conversation where it first surfaces, to a complete plan, to the people, legal home, and funding that let it last.

Our vision is a world where a good idea is limited only by its merit—not by paperwork, capital, or know-how.

02 · The Problem

Good ideas stall before they start.

Someone has an idea for a charitable institution: a music festival, a community archive, a research program. To raise money, donors need a tax deduction and foundations need a recognized charity. Forming one takes months and real cost: incorporation, a board, bylaws, an IRS application, accounting, insurance, and ongoing compliance.

A traditional fiscal sponsor solves the legal half and little else. The founder is still left to work out which programs to run, which roles to fill, how to raise money, and what to do first. Many good ideas stop here, long before they are tested.

04 · How It Works

From first conversation to sponsored project.

  1. Agreements

    The founder signs a fiscal sponsorship agreement with the nonprofit arm and an engagement agreement for institution building. An engagement lead is assigned.

  2. Consultation

    The lead captures the idea in the founder’s own words. Every later conclusion traces back to that record.

  3. Blueprint

    FSOS drafts the complete plan: mission, programs, audiences, legal requirements, funding model, and timeline. Every value states its basis, and open questions carry sensible defaults, so nothing waits.

  4. Workforce

    Once the founder confirms the blueprint, an AI workforce is assembled for the institution. A Chief of Staff plans the work; specialists research and draft; the lead reviews every draft.

  5. Sponsorship

    The nonprofit arm accepts the project into sponsorship. From then on, every contract, hire, gift, and payment is approved by the founder and the Sponsor, and carried out by a person.

05 · Principles

Agents draft. People decide.

Proposed until confirmed
Anything the AI produces is marked proposed, with its source. Only the founder’s decision makes it confirmed.
Every value has a source
Each fact in a blueprint says who or what produced it, and on what basis.
Nothing consequential without approval
Agents never sign, pay, submit, or publish. Those steps go to the founder and, where required, the Sponsor.
Walls between institutions
Each institution’s workspace is visible only to the engagement lead assigned to it.

06 · The Model

Two arms, one standard.

The Legal Home

Nonprofit Arm

Fiscal sponsor to every project. It holds the money, accepts gifts, signs contracts, and keeps each project within the rules for charities. Founders lead the work; the Sponsor keeps legal and financial control.

The Builders

For-Profit Arm

Provides institution building through its engagement leads and FSOS. It has no authority over a project’s money, and it pays for FSOS, every AI cost included, from its own funds.

Information crosses between the arms only on purpose, and project donations never pay for our software.

A Mulvium Company

FSOS

FSOS, the FiscalSponsor Operating System, is the software our engagement leads use to turn a founder’s idea into an operating institution. From a first consultation it drafts the institution’s complete plan, the blueprint: its mission, programs, audiences, legal and operational requirements, funding model, timeline, and the decisions still open. Every value states its basis—stated by the founder, inferred, or assumed—so nothing is invented without saying so, and no work waits on an answer: open questions carry sensible defaults until the founder decides.

From the blueprint, FSOS assembles an AI workforce organized like a small, real organization. A Chief of Staff turns goals into tasks and sequences the work; specialists research, analyze, and draft. For the Beethoven festival1, that is thirteen agents: the Chief of Staff at the center of the circle above, and twelve specialists around it. Every agent is drawn from an approved catalogue, holds only the tools its job requires, and hands its drafts to the engagement lead for review. A permanent, sourced memory records everything decided and learned.

Agents draft; people decide. When work reaches a step with real-world effect—signing a contract, accepting a gift, submitting a grant, spending money—the agent does not act. It files a request tied to the exact draft. The founder approves it, the Sponsor approves it where required, and a person carries it out. Founders never log in to FSOS: they meet their engagement lead and receive finished work.

1 The Beethoven festival is FSOS’s reference case: a founder’s idea for “a three-day festival dedicated to Beethoven’s solo, chamber, and orchestral music, including panel discussions and intellectual discourse.” FSOS is built and tested end to end against it.
