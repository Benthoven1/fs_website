Mulvium Sponsorship Operating System

MulviumSOS

The software that builds an institution around a founder’s idea, and makes it known.

M.SOS, for short, is what our engagement leads work in. It has two tools on one core. Both work from the same record of the institution and answer to the same rules, which is what makes M.SOS an operating system rather than two programs.

Atelier Plans and builds the institution Twelve agents under a Chief of Staff Grove Gives it a public voice Eight agents, directed by an account lead

1

Atelier

Where the institution is planned and built.

From a first consultation, Atelier drafts the institution’s complete plan: the blueprint. Every value states its basis, so nothing is invented without saying so, and no work waits on an answer: open questions carry sensible defaults until the founder decides.

From the blueprint, Atelier assembles an AI workforce organized like a small, real organization. Every agent is drawn from an approved catalogue, holds only the tools its job requires, and hands its drafts to a person for review. A permanent, sourced memory records everything decided and learned.

Grove

Where the institution finds its public voice.

Being seen is one of the largest costs of building an institution, and most of an agency retainer pays for hours of production. Grove’s team does that production, directed by an account lead, so a full public relations program costs a fraction of a traditional retainer.

For the projects we sponsor

Grove can be added to a sponsorship, so a new institution is heard from its first day.

For organizations outside sponsorship

Grove can be retained on its own. The organization approves its own statements.

It is the public relations people already know: the press, an institution’s own channels, awards, and care in a crisis. Clients work with it as they would with any firm. Their account lead presents the work in person, and the client’s decisions are recorded at the meeting, often a month of the calendar at once.

One media directory and one awards directory serve every client, holding only public, professional facts, so research done once benefits everyone and no reporter hears from Grove too often. Each client’s own work stays walled off from the others.

3

An operating system

Each layer deals only with its neighbors.

A computer works in layers. A person uses an application; the application runs on the operating system; and the operating system runs the hardware, the machine itself. So the person never has to manage the machine, and no application can misuse it.

M.SOS stands in the same place. The founder, through their lead, works with Atelier and Grove. Atelier and Grove run on M.SOS, which keeps the institution’s record, runs its agents, and asks a person before anything real happens. And M.SOS runs on the sponsor: Mulvium’s nonprofit, which holds the funds, signs the contracts, and answers to the law. Like hardware, the sponsor is what finally acts in the world.

That is why the two tools share so much: one record, one set of rules, one sponsor beneath them. A new tool would run on M.SOS the same way, as a new application runs on the same operating system. For an organization Grove serves outside sponsorship, its own board and accounts take the sponsor’s place at the bottom.

4

Agents draft; people decide.

When work reaches a step with real-world effect, the agent does not act.

  • Signing a contract
  • Accepting a gift
  • Submitting a grant
  • Spending money
  • Speaking in public

  1. Draft

    An agent prepares the work and stops short of acting.

  2. Request

    It files a request tied to the exact draft.

  3. Verify

    Every factual claim in public copy cites a confirmed record or a verified source, checked before anyone reviews it.

  4. Approve

    The founder or client approves, and the sponsor too, where required.

  5. Act

    Only the approved words go out, at their scheduled time.

Founders and clients never log in to M.SOS: they meet their lead and receive finished work.
