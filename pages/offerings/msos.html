Mulvium Sponsorship Operating System

MulviumSOS

MulviumSOS, or M.SOS for short, is the software our engagement leads use to build an institution around a founder’s idea, and to make it known. It has two tools on one core: Atelier, where the institution is planned and built, and Grove, where it finds its public voice. Both work from the same record of the institution and answer to the same rules, which is what makes M.SOS an operating system rather than two programs.

Atelier

Atelier builds the institution. From a first consultation it drafts the institution’s complete plan, the blueprint: its mission, programs, audiences, legal and operational requirements, funding model, timeline, and the decisions still open. Every value states its basis—stated by the founder, inferred, or assumed—so nothing is invented without saying so, and no work waits on an answer: open questions carry sensible defaults until the founder decides.

From the blueprint, Atelier assembles an AI workforce organized like a small, real organization, under a Chief of Staff who turns goals into tasks and sequences the work: research, grants, development, finance, governance, compliance, programs, and evaluation. Every agent is drawn from an approved catalogue, holds only the tools its job requires, and hands its drafts to a person for review. A permanent, sourced memory records everything decided and learned.

Grove

Grove makes the institution known. Being seen is one of the largest costs of building an institution, and most of an agency retainer pays for hours of production. Grove’s team does that production—brand and storytelling, media relations, content, social, the mailing list, awards, crisis, and reporting—directed by an account lead, so a full public relations program costs a fraction of a traditional retainer.

Grove serves the projects we sponsor, which can add it to their sponsorship, and organizations outside sponsorship, which can retain it on its own and approve their own statements. It is the public relations people already know: the press, an institution’s own channels, awards, and care in a crisis.

Clients work with it as they would with any firm. Their account lead presents the work in person, and the client’s decisions are recorded at the meeting, often a month of the calendar at once. One media directory and one awards directory serve every client, holding only public, professional facts, so research done once benefits everyone and no reporter hears from Grove too often. Each client’s own work stays walled off from the others.

An operating system

A computer works in layers. A person uses an application; the application runs on the operating system; and the operating system runs the hardware, the machine itself. Each layer deals only with its neighbors, so the person never has to manage the machine, and no application can misuse it.

M.SOS stands in the same place. The founder, through their lead, works with Atelier and Grove. Atelier and Grove run on M.SOS, which keeps the institution’s record, runs its agents, and asks a person before anything real happens. And M.SOS runs on the sponsor: Mulvium’s nonprofit, which holds the funds, signs the contracts, and answers to the law. Like hardware, the sponsor is what finally acts in the world.

That is why the two tools share so much: one record, one set of rules, one sponsor beneath them. A new tool would run on M.SOS the same way, as a new application runs on the same operating system. For an organization Grove serves outside sponsorship, its own board and accounts take the sponsor’s place at the bottom.

People decide

Agents draft; people decide. When work reaches a step with real-world effect—signing a contract, accepting a gift, submitting a grant, spending money, speaking in public—the agent does not act. It files a request tied to the exact draft. Every factual claim in public copy cites a confirmed record or a verified source, checked before anyone reviews it. The founder or client approves, the sponsor approves where required, and only the approved words go out, at their scheduled time. Founders and clients never log in to M.SOS: they meet their lead and receive finished work.
