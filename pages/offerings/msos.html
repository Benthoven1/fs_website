being seen

A public relations agency costs $5,000 to $25,000 a month.

Most of a retainer pays for production hours: writing, building media lists, monitoring, formatting reports. Few new institutions can pay it, so most are never heard of.

A large field, poorly served

  • 98,417new charities recognized by the IRS in 2023
  • 12,184projects under just 100 fiscal sponsors, who steward $2.6 billion in gifts and grants
  • $24.6 billionearned by U.S. public relations firms in 2025

Sources: IRS Data Book, as reported by MinistryWatch; 2023 Fiscal Sponsor Field Scan (100 responding sponsors); IBISWorld.

Software does the production. People make the decisions.

M.SOS drafts an institution’s complete plan from a first consultation, then puts an AI workforce to the research, analysis, and drafting the plan calls for. Our staff review the work, the founder decides, and the sponsor signs. This is the workspace our staff see, live.

Agents draft; people decide.

An agent researches and writes, and stops. It never sends, signs, pays, submits, or publishes. Anything with an effect in the world waits for a person, and every fact in public copy cites its source.

    Turns an idea into an operating institution.

    What a client sees, every month

    Each Grove client’s lead presents the month from this dashboard: how often the institution was seen, how much of the conversation it held, and whether its messages landed.

    Why it is hard to copy

    • A record, not a chat Everything about an institution is kept as a structured, sourced record. Screens, briefs, and every agent’s context are views of it.
    • Accountable by design Agents come only from an approved catalogue, approvals follow a legal matrix, and a tamper-evident trail records every step.
    • Memory that compounds What a client accepted, and what worked last month, guides the next draft. Each institution makes M.SOS better at the next.

    Why an operating system

    Atelier and Grove are parts of M.SOS, as a scheduler and a network stack are parts of an operating system: they share one core, one record, and one set of rules, and the sponsor beneath them finally acts. Point to a layer to find its counterpart.
