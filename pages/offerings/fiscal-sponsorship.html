01

What is a fiscal sponsor?

An established nonprofit that takes a new charitable project under its legal umbrella.

The project becomes one of the sponsor’s own programs, and that one step changes everything. Gifts to it become tax-deductible, foundations can fund it, and its founders can begin the work now, instead of first spending months and real money forming a nonprofit of their own.

Mulvium’s nonprofit arm serves as that sponsor. Founders keep leading the work day to day; Mulvium carries the legal and financial responsibilities.

Getting started

Gifts

02

How does it work?

Money and responsibility move along one simple path.

Donors People, businesses, foundations, and public grants
Tax-deductible gifts and grants Receipts and reports
Mulvium Holds the project’s own fund, signs its contracts, and keeps it compliant
Funds for approved expenses Plans and requests
Your project Its founders lead the work

  1. Apply

    Tell us about the project. We confirm its purpose is charitable and that it is a good fit.

  2. Agree

    A fiscal sponsorship agreement makes the project a Mulvium program, with a fund of its own.

  3. Raise

    Donors and foundations give to Mulvium for the project. Every gift is receipted and held for the project alone.

  4. Build

    You lead the work. Mulvium signs the contracts, pays approved expenses, and keeps the books; an engagement lead and FSOS help plan the rest.

  5. Grow

    Stay under Mulvium’s umbrella as long as it serves the project, or move to a home of your own. Funds raised for the project stay with its charitable purpose.

In plain terms

Held in a separate fund, spent only on the project’s purpose, and never mixed with anyone’s personal money.

It signs, accepts gifts, issues receipts, and answers to regulators. Founders lead the work and recommend what it needs.

Like every fiscal sponsor, Mulvium charges a fee, set in the sponsorship agreement and typically a share of the funds received.

Four projects, four domains

Hypothetical projects, each the kind of idea fiscal sponsorship exists for.

2027 · A multi-day festival

The Beethoven Bicentennial Festival

2027 is the 200th anniversary of Beethoven’s death in 1827. This is the perfect opportunity to celebrate his universal, invigorating music in a unique way. The vision is to produce a multi-day festival featuring solo, chamber, vocal, and orchestral music, including intellectual discourse with scholars and students. The hope is to collaborate with the local community and invite distinguished musicians from around the world.

It needs venues · musicians · travel · sponsors · audiences

From there, Mulvium can

  1. Accept tax-deductible gifts, sponsorships, and grants for the festival, and send every donor a receipt.
  2. Sign the venue, artist, and travel contracts, so no founder signs personally.
  3. Hold the festival’s fund and pay musicians, halls, and vendors as expenses are approved.
  4. Insure the concerts and keep the festival within the rules for charities.
  5. Draft the festival’s plan with FSOS: programming, budget, funders, and timeline.

Year-round · Community science

The River Census

Rivers change faster than anyone measures them. The vision is a volunteer network that samples one river every month—temperature, dissolved oxygen, nutrients, and microplastics—and publishes every measurement as open data. Local high schools adopt sampling sites, and a university lab checks the methods and helps read the results.

It needs equipment · lab analysis · volunteers · site access · data hosting

From there, Mulvium can

  1. Apply for science and environmental grants as the legal grantee, and report back to funders.
  2. Buy and hold the sampling equipment, and pay for laboratory analysis.
  3. Sign a methods and data-sharing agreement with the university lab.
  4. Insure volunteers in the field and arrange permission to reach the sampling sites.
  5. Keep the books, so every dollar can be traced to a measurement.

Weekly · Free for families

The Saturday Math Circle

Some of the best mathematicians start as curious kids with no one to ask. The vision is a free Saturday math circle for middle schoolers—puzzles, proofs, and games led by volunteer graduate students and retired engineers—meeting at a public library, with a shelf of problem books anyone can borrow.

It needs space · volunteers · books · snacks · transit passes

From there, Mulvium can

  1. Sign the library’s facility agreement and pay for books and materials.
  2. Screen and background-check every volunteer who works with children, under a written child-safety policy.
  3. Accept gifts from families, alumni, and local businesses, and apply for education grants.
  4. Insure the sessions and keep attendance and spending records.
  5. Plan the circle’s growth with FSOS: curriculum, volunteer roster, and a budget for a second site.

Weekly route · A mobile service

Clean Start Laundry

A clean set of clothes opens doors: to a job interview, a classroom, a shelter bed. The vision is a mobile laundry and shower van that visits shelters and encampments on a weekly route, staffed by a paid driver and rotating volunteers, offering free washes and a few unhurried minutes of dignity.

It needs a van · washers · water hookups · a driver · permits

From there, Mulvium can

  1. Accept the donated van and equipment as in-kind gifts, with receipts for the donors.
  2. Hire and pay the driver through payroll, with the protections an employer provides.
  3. Obtain the vehicle’s insurance and the permits for each stop on the route.
  4. Sign agreements with shelters for parking and water hookups.
  5. Track every dollar for donors and for grant reports.
