FSOS

FSOS, the FiscalSponsor Operating System, is the software our engagement leads use to turn a founder’s idea into an operating institution. From a first consultation it drafts the institution’s complete plan, the blueprint: its mission, programs, audiences, legal and operational requirements, funding model, timeline, and the decisions still open. Every value states its basis—stated by the founder, inferred, or assumed—so nothing is invented without saying so, and no work waits on an answer: open questions carry sensible defaults until the founder decides.

From the blueprint, FSOS assembles an AI workforce organized like a small, real organization: two teams under one Chief of Staff, who turns goals into tasks and sequences the work. The first team builds and runs the institution—research, grants, development, finance, governance, compliance, programs, evaluation. The second is a public relations and communications agency. Every agent is drawn from an approved catalogue, holds only the tools its job requires, and hands its drafts to a person for review. A permanent, sourced memory records everything decided and learned.

The agency

Being seen is one of the largest costs of building an institution, and most of an agency retainer pays for hours of production. FSOS’s second team does that production—brand and storytelling, media relations, content, social, the mailing list, awards, crisis, and reporting—directed by an account lead, so a full public relations program costs a fraction of a traditional retainer.

The agency serves the projects we sponsor, which can add it to their sponsorship, and organizations outside sponsorship, which can retain it on its own and approve their own statements. It is the public relations people already know: the press, an institution’s own channels, awards, and care in a crisis.

Clients work with it as they would with any firm. Their account lead presents the work in person, and the client’s decisions are recorded at the meeting, often a month of the calendar at once. One media directory and one awards directory serve every client, holding only public, professional facts, so research done once benefits everyone and no reporter hears from the firm too often. Each client’s own work stays walled off from the others.

People decide

Agents draft; people decide. When work reaches a step with real-world effect—signing a contract, accepting a gift, submitting a grant, spending money, speaking in public—the agent does not act. It files a request tied to the exact draft. Every factual claim in public copy cites a confirmed record or a verified source, checked before anyone reviews it. The founder or client approves, the Sponsor approves where required, and only the approved words go out, at their scheduled time. Founders and clients never log in to FSOS: they meet their lead and receive finished work.
