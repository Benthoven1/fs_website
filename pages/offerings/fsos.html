FSOS

FSOS, the FiscalSponsor Operating System, is the software our engagement leads use to turn a founder’s idea into an operating institution. From a first consultation it drafts the institution’s complete plan, the blueprint: its mission, programs, audiences, legal and operational requirements, funding model, timeline, and the decisions still open. Every value states its basis—stated by the founder, inferred, or assumed—so nothing is invented without saying so, and no work waits on an answer: open questions carry sensible defaults until the founder decides.

From the blueprint, FSOS assembles an AI workforce organized like a small, real organization. A Chief of Staff turns goals into tasks and sequences the work; specialists research, analyze, and draft. For the Beethoven festival1, that is thirteen agents: the Chief of Staff at the center of the circle above, and twelve specialists around it. Every agent is drawn from an approved catalogue, holds only the tools its job requires, and hands its drafts to the engagement lead for review. A permanent, sourced memory records everything decided and learned.

Agents draft; people decide. When work reaches a step with real-world effect—signing a contract, accepting a gift, submitting a grant, spending money—the agent does not act. It files a request tied to the exact draft. The founder approves it, the Sponsor approves it where required, and a person carries it out. Founders never log in to FSOS: they meet their engagement lead and receive finished work.

1 The Beethoven festival is FSOS’s reference case: a founder’s idea for “a three-day festival dedicated to Beethoven’s solo, chamber, and orchestral music, including panel discussions and intellectual discourse.” FSOS is built and tested end to end against it.
