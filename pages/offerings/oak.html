Oak

Cherish authentic human connection.

Scroll

Oak’s dual mandate

  1. 1Increase real-world human connection.
  2. 2Beautify the modern world. The mandate Oak shares with all of Mulvium, and the one its sponsored projects serve.

Oak is a mobile app for the people you talk with most: friends, partners, mentors, co-founders, book clubs. With everyone’s consent, it listens to a conversation and keeps only what was meaningful—a sharp quote, an obscure fact or recommendation, a half-formed idea—as a tree you can return to and share.

1

Interesting information, and lots of it.

You and three friends are talking about the 2026 World Cup. You mention how you wanted Norway to win, then someone mentions Norway has the largest sovereign wealth fund in the world. One friend wanted Japan to win, and the conversation shifts to her favorite Japanese films, including Seven Samurai from 1954 and Kwaidan from 1965. Another wanted Cape Verde to win, given they were major underdogs. The last friend wishes the Philippines had made it, because he thinks they’re the most remarkable underdogs in history. He talks about the Philippines in World War II, including the Battle of Leyte Gulf, and Taffy 3, the tin-can sailors who held off an entire Japanese fleet. You ask which myth the story of Taffy 3 resembles, and the group agrees: David and Goliath.

An hour later, you and your friends forgot the specifics.

Great conversations should not be forgotten. They are essential to personal growth, and the building blocks of a life’s meaning.

2

The current options fall short

  1. Voice memos and ambient pendants record entire conversations The cost: Privacy
  2. AI note takers are too formal The cost: Aesthetic
  3. Pen and paper is too laborious The cost: Efficiency

And none of them can be shared, or shows a conversation’s shape: where it starts, where it goes, and how it gets there.

4

Each conversation grows a tree

As you talk, Oak draws the conversation the way it happened, so its shape is there to see long after the details would have faded.

  1. Topics become branches

  2. Meaningful moments become acorns

  3. Rich acorns attract a chickadee, who leads you to the Wikipedia article

5

Many trees make a forest

A record that grows over the years, in which ideas and patterns become visible.

  1. Revisit an old tree, and start the conversation where you left off.
  2. Share a tree with the people you spoke with.
  3. Export everything. Your trees are kept on your device, and never used to train models or for analysis.

6

Privacy is the product

Nothing is kept on a server

Audio is transcribed as it is heard, analyzed in real time, and discarded continuously. Neither the transcript nor what Oak makes of it is stored or used for training. Your trees and forests export to your own device, so they are never proprietary, and never training data.

No one is recorded without consent

To begin, the other person receives a code on their own phone and reads it to you, as a rider gives a driver a PIN to start a ride. In return, they receive their own copy of the conversation tree, waiting for them when they make an Oak account.
