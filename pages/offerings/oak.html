Oak

Cherish authentic human connection.

Scroll

You and three friends are talking about the 2026 World Cup. You mention how you wanted Norway to win, then someone mentions Norway has the largest sovereign wealth fund in the world. One friend wanted Japan to win, and the conversation shifts to her favorite Japanese films, including Seven Samurai from 1954 and Kwaidan from 1965. Another wanted Cape Verde to win, given they were major underdogs. The last friend wishes the Philippines had made it, because he thinks they’re the most remarkable underdogs in history. He talks about the Philippines in World War II, including the Battle of Leyte Gulf, and Taffy 3, the tin-can sailors who held off an entire Japanese fleet. You ask which myth the story of Taffy 3 resembles, and the group agrees: David and Goliath.

An hour later, you and your friends forgot the specifics.

Great conversations should not be forgotten. They are essential to personal growth, and the building blocks of a life’s meaning.

Yet every way of keeping one asks for a compromise. Voice memos and ambient pendants record everything, and give up privacy. AI note takers turn a dinner into minutes, and give up beauty. Pen and paper make you stop talking, and give up ease. None of them can be shared, and none shows a conversation’s shape: where it starts, where it goes, and how it gets there.

Oak keeps what mattered, and lets the rest go. It is a mobile app for the people you talk with most: friends, partners, mentors, co-founders, book clubs. With everyone’s consent it listens, and as you talk it draws the conversation the way it happened. Here is that evening, as Oak would keep it.

Over months and years, trees make a forest, and ideas and patterns that no single evening shows become visible. Revisit an old tree and pick up where you left off. Share one with the people who were there. Export everything, whenever you like.

Most people are curious; few have tools made for it. Among U.S. adults:

77% research topics that interest them

18% use AI to do it

58% take and organize notes

15% use AI to do it

Menlo Ventures, 2025

Privacy is the product. Audio is transcribed as it is heard, analyzed in real time, and discarded continuously. Neither the transcript nor what Oak makes of it is stored or used for training, and nothing persists on a server. Your trees export to your own device, so they are never proprietary, and never training data.

4827

No one is recorded without consent. To begin, the other person receives a code on their own phone and reads it to you, as a rider gives a driver a PIN to start a ride. In return, their own copy of the tree waits for them when they make an Oak account.

Oak is in development. We would like to hear from people who love a good conversation.
