values

Beauty can be all around us.

We need beauty now more than ever, and not only in art: in a well-made tool, a clear argument, a kept promise, a street worth walking down. Beauty that inspires future generations to challenge and improve the world. And we need new role models: master craftsmen, polymaths, critical thinkers.

Beauty is vanishing

Most things that are profoundly beautiful are wrapped up in the canon of the past and in the splendor of the natural world. Why can’t we find this level of beauty in our globalized, cosmopolitan lives: in what we build, how we work, how we argue, and how we treat one another?

Where can we find beauty untainted by polemical outcry, economic structure, technological mediation, or social stratification? Something beautiful in its craftsmanship, its subtlety, and its ability to inspire the deepest and most radiant feelings?

Today, the political and technological classes obscure beauty more than they shape it.

Four things worth making more of

The aesthetic > over the grotesque

With the coming infinitude of AI-made everything, from pictures and songs to products and opinions, everyone will consume exactly what they want, and society will tolerate eclecticism less and less. People will forget what intellectual discomfort truly feels like. We choose the beauty that asks something of us, in a proof as much as a poem: craftsmanship, subtlety, and difference.

If people can’t tolerate aesthetic differences, how will people tolerate each other?

The sublime > over the spectacle

Mass consumption does not promote intellectual vitality; it promotes complacency. The live experience economy cannot catch up, and has little reason not to follow the trends of the internet and the culture industry. We choose the high and the pure: work, ideas and places made to be stood before, not scrolled past.

Entertainment is not necessarily beautiful.

Role models > over parasocial icons

We need new role models: master craftsmen, polymaths, and critical thinkers, the kind who once held a post like Bach’s, writing music for regular service. In 1603 the Hamburg scholar Johannes Wower defined perfect polymathy as “wandering freely and with unbridled speed through all the fields of the disciplines.”

Master craftsmen. Polymaths. Critical thinkers.

Myth & epic > over conspiracy

The legend of Kitezh tells of a thirteenth-century Russian city that sank beneath a lake during the Mongol invasion. It remains invisible, yet people can still hear its carillons and canticles. Its theme is natural harmony: the celestial found in the deepest places of the earth. Stories like it bind a people together, where conspiracy only divides them.

The world and the heavens are both beautiful.

Reimagine social responsibility

Three people, each asked for something. Choose one.

the patron

Accumulated wealth should power the frontier, inexorably tied to human achievement.

There needs to be a funnel where personal wealth comes in and global impact comes out, where creative ideas and the creative process can be incubated. This is not about the wealthy selling a vision of the world to consumers; it is about altering the cultural consciousness.

the maker

Creativity turned toward problem solving, not only toward self-expression.

Through polymathy, and through collaboration between humanists and technologists. Scientists and engineers, artists and architects, each designing for a single intelligent whole that is greater than the sum of its parts.

the citizen

A framework of social humility, to reconcile divisiveness and engender harmony.

It is impossible to avoid suffering entirely. The hope is to help the world stop and smell the roses, one beautiful thing at a time.

Power the frontier, not the simulacrum.
