values

Make more beautiful things

Mulvium’s mandate is to beautify the modern world: to cultivate, replicate, and proliferate new beautiful things. Four values say what that means. Each is an inequality.

  • The aesthetic>the grotesque
  • The sublime>the spectacle
  • Role models>parasocial icons
  • Myth & epic>conspiracy

Why beauty

Beauty asks to be copied. A song is sung again, a garden planted again, a story told to the next generation. Make one beautiful thing, and others will want to make more. That is why Mulvium builds institutions: an institution is a way of making beautiful things again and again, long after the first time.

The same is true of what is not beautiful. The grotesque, the spectacle, the parasocial and the conspiratorial replicate too, and today they replicate faster. So each of our values is an inequality. The second term is not banished; every culture makes both. It is outranked. What a culture chooses to multiply is the question.

The aesthetic > over the grotesque

Restoration of the world around you

The grotesque is made to arrest attention: the shocking, the ugly for effect, whatever a feed rewards because it cannot be ignored. It asks for a reaction and is forgotten by the next one. The aesthetic asks for attention and repays it. You return to a symphony, a garden, a well-made room, and find more each time.

“Why don’t we celebrate the beautiful and the authentic over the grotesque and the artificial?”From the founder’s letter

in practice The Beethoven Bicentennial Festival A multi-day festival of solo, chamber, vocal, and orchestral music for the 200th anniversary of Beethoven’s death.

The sublime > over the spectacle

Curiosity in the awe-some and the original

The sublime is what exceeds us: a total eclipse, a cathedral, a night sky far from any city. It is felt in person and in company, and it leaves us smaller and larger at once. The spectacle is its image, consumed alone through a screen, and it asks nothing of us but the next one.

The sublime holds the place of the spirit among our values, open to the devout and the secular alike. We choose to gather people where awe actually happens.

in practice Six Minutes at Luxor Six minutes of totality over the temples of Luxor, and a festival of learning that builds toward them.

Role models > over parasocial icons

Feelings of connection and empathy

A parasocial icon is known by millions and knows none of them. A role model is someone whose life you can watch at close range: a teacher, a coach, a mentor, a neighbor. They know your name. They correct you, and you can grow into them.

“A neighbor is not just someone you live next to. It is anyone who is in need or whom you encounter, regardless of their background or beliefs.”From the founder’s letter

in practice The Last Lectures A town’s old high school closes with a last day of lectures, given by every living teacher who taught there.

Myth & epic > over conspiracy

Love of truth

Myth and conspiracy answer the same hunger: for a story large enough to explain our place in the world. Conspiracy answers it with suspicion, and isolates whoever believes it. Myth and epic answer it with meaning that is shared and handed down, and bind a people together: David and Goliath, the Odyssey, the Hudhud chants of the Ifugao.

We choose to keep the old stories sung, and to tell new ones worth handing down.

in practice The Hudhud Harvest A harvest festival in the rice terraces of the northern Philippines, where elders still sing the Hudhud.

Chosen by people

No algorithm can rank these terms for us. Mulvium’s software drafts and organizes, but what is worth making is chosen by people, in the real world, one project at a time. That curation is the work we care about most.

“Because one neighbor at a time, we make the world a place worth living.”From the founder’s letter
