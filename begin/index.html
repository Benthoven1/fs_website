What you receive first

  • A blueprint. Your institution’s complete plan: mission, programs, audiences, funding model, timeline, and the decisions still open. Each entry says where it came from.
  • First drafts, with sources. Such as a 90-day founding plan, budget scenarios, and a map of likely funders.
  • Help reaching patrons. Your team drafts appeals and sponsorship tiers for your approval.

You approve first. Mulvium signs.

  • Signing a contract, accepting a gift, submitting a grant, making a payment: each waits for your approval of the exact text, then Mulvium’s. If the text changes, you approve again.
  • You never log in to software. You meet your lead and receive finished work.
  • Gifts are held by Mulvium in a separate fund for your project and spent only on its purpose. The software never moves money.

Plain answers

Is my project a fit?

Yes, if its purpose is charitable, educational, artistic, scientific or otherwise public-benefit, it is based in the US, and you are glad for Mulvium to hold the money and sign. No track record needed. Not for private profit or political campaigns.

What does it cost?

One fee: 10% of gifts received, set out in the sponsorship agreement. You are never billed for the work of building your organization.

Who will I talk to?

Only Mulvium. First a conversation about you and your idea; if it fits, one agreement with Mulvium, and then your engagement lead.

Where do things stand?

Mulvium is not open yet. Before we form The Mulvium Foundation and apply for recognition as a 501(c)(3) public charity, we want to hear from the founders it would serve. The software behind the service is built and tested. Until Mulvium opens, it cannot accept gifts for a project.
